OpenGym

Live occupancy for Carnegie Mellon's gyms, so students can skip the crowd, not the workout. OpenGym already existed. I'm redesigning its user flow, its interface and the logic behind what it shows, together with the hardware team that produces its live counts.

BEFORE · THE EXISTING GYM PAGE

01 / WHERE IT STARTEDTHE EXISTING PRODUCT

One page per gym.

  1. 01Live count.

    How many people are inside out of the gym's capacity, shown as a gauge, with the reading's status and when the gym next opens or closes.

  2. 02Typical busyness.

    The busiest and least busy hours on average, and an hour-by-hour chart for each day of the week against today's measurements.

  3. 03The place.

    Operating hours, the address, photos of each area with what it offers, and a map.

02 / WHAT THE REDESIGN TACKLES

When the numbers can't be trusted.

  1. 01No data looks like no people.

    With the counter offline, the page still reads “0 of 94 inside” beside an empty gauge, which looks like an empty gym rather than a missing reading.

  2. 02Summaries that contradict.

    With too little data, the busiest and the least busy hours both come out as 9am – 12pm, “about 0% full”.

  3. 03Logic shared with hardware.

    What counts as live, late or offline is decided together with the hardware team, so the interface can say which one the reading is and what to trust.
