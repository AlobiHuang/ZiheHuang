Kumi

Kumi is an AI assistant for anyone whose work is spread across too many apps. It gathers your calendar, inbox, deadlines and tools into one small window, sums up what needs you next, and keeps each app already open one tap away, so moving between platforms stops costing time. I designed it and directed its build with Claude Code over three weeks.

High-level goals that defined my design

  1. 01One list across platforms.

    Everything due, from every app, in one list sorted by when it’s due.

  2. 02Colour by kind and urgency.

    The hue says what kind of thing it is; the amount of colour says how soon it needs you.

  3. 03Read in one glance.

    Small enough to keep open beside your work, so a look is all it takes to know what’s next.

Kumi puts every app you check on one board you design.

One window, the size of a chat box, that you can keep open beside your work.

Needs you now

One list of deadlines, events, replies and your own reminders from every connected app, sorted only by when they’re due, one line each. Done, Snooze and Dismiss each come with Undo, and every row has Open in … to jump to the app. The headline counts only what needs you right now: “2 things need you now.”

Colour that means something

The hue says what kind of thing it is. The amount of colour says how much it needs you. A board shows one or two solid items at most, so the eye goes straight to them, and kind is always shown by an icon and a word too, never by colour alone.

  • TimeCalendar, Zoom, gym bookings
  • PeopleGmail, iMessage, Slack

Everything already on the calendar

Opened from Needs you now, the calendar holds every deadline, event, reply and gym session from your apps, already placed by time. Nothing is added by hand, which is the part people give up on in Google Calendar.

Built like Lego

The board is a grid of square cells. Widgets drag and resize, and the ones you land on move along to the next free spot and slide back if you change your mind. The window comes in three sizes, landscape or portrait, and each size has its own layout that fills it exactly. Every drag also has a one-click version, and every widget moves with the keyboard.

Wherever you look

The same board as a read-only desktop wallpaper or an always-on screen, in a dark theme, and as one column with a tab bar on a phone, light or dark.

Usable by everyone

Kumi targets WCAG 2.2 AA. Every page works by keyboard alone, contrast is measured in both themes, charts come with a table a screen reader can read, and undo messages never time out. 132 automated tests run accessibility checks on every page, in both themes and on desktop, tablet and phone.

Explorations

  • From a full screen to a window.

    The first version filled the screen in a calm matcha theme. Across the whole screen it was too much to take in at a glance, so Kumi became a small window: compact, informative and clean.

  • Three ways to wear the same colours.

    Sticker, Poster and Gummy share one colour system and differ in how loud it gets. Gummy kept the warmth with the least noise: soft same-hue gradients, only on what needs you now. The working name then was Tessu; Kumi, from kumihimo, Japanese braiding of many threads into one cord, won.

  • An intro that shows the real apps.

    The intro first cycled through abstract colours and textures for each app. It now shows each app’s own icon with the K standing in for its symbol, so “one board, every app” reads in a second.

Next Steps

  1. 01Connect real accounts.

    Every app runs on realistic sample data for now. Next is sign-in and the real connections; iMessage, Grubhub and LinkedIn have no public API, so the Sources page says how each one would connect.

  2. 02Test with students.

    A week of real use with a few students, to see whether one glance really replaces the round of apps.

  3. 03A screen reader pass.

    A full pass with NVDA, then testing with people who use assistive technology every day.
