CMUsed

A second-hand marketplace for the Carnegie Mellon community, where students buy from and sell to each other in one place: browse by category, contact the seller directly, and list an item in three short steps. I joined it as its product designer, to make it easier to use from end to end.

Why CMUsed?

Students trade in group chats and marketplaces built for strangers. Listings get buried, and you can’t tell who’s selling.

CMUsed is one campus marketplace, sorted by what students sell, where every account is a verified CMU email.

Reading the dashboard.

Before changing anything, I read the admin dashboard to find where the marketplace actually breaks. Five findings, from the day I joined (Sept 24) to Oct 20, 2026.

Sign-ups weren’t the problem.

In the 30 days to Oct 20, 100 people signed up and 7 new listings went up: about 14 sign-ups for every listing. The week I joined it was 55 to 3. People arrive; very few of them sell.

A few sellers carry the supply.

Only 173 of 1,822 accounts have ever listed something, and 83 have sold. Seven accounts posted about a third of every listing on the site (279 of 876) and 37% of everything sold (152 of 407).

Buyers write, and mostly hear nothing.

355 buyers started a conversation. 41 got a reply, after a median of 28 hours, and 13 turned into a back-and-forth. 96 of the 110 sellers who were contacted never answered.

The chat doesn’t show where deals happen.

407 items are marked sold, yet only 41 conversations ever got a reply. Either most deals are arranged somewhere else, or the chats are gone: chats idle for 90 days are deleted, and the dashboard’s buyer count is only an estimate. So the reply rate describes the chat, not the deal, and I read it with that in mind.

Sales have stalled.

The sold count went from 403 on Sept 24 to 407 on Oct 20: four sales in almost four weeks, while 469 listings worth $33.6k sit unsold. Monthly active users fell from 288 to 222 over the same weeks, most likely the start-of-semester rush fading; a year of readings would confirm it.

What the problem actually was.

Not sign-ups, and not demand for what is listed: almost half of all listings have sold. CMUsed depends on a handful of sellers, most buyers’ messages go unanswered, and sales have nearly stopped. So the work starts on the seller side: reaching the people who have things to sell, and making sure buyers hear back.

What the data can’t tell me yet.

  • Why 90% never list. They may only want to buy, or they start a listing and give up. The dashboard doesn’t record listings started, so a shorter listing flow stays a hypothesis until it does.
  • Where deals happen. A one-tap question when an item is marked sold, “Sold through CMUsed?”, would show whether the chat matters.
  • What normal looks like. One semester of readings can’t separate a seasonal dip from a real decline.

Solutions?

Low activation.

A shorter path to a first listing.

From browsing and searching to contacting a seller or selling an item.

Low seller response rate.

Real-time updates through notifications.

Email notifications now, then notifications on the phone once the app is out.

Thin supply.

Seasonal sales and promotions.

Promoting move-in/move-out, Halloween and Christmas sales.

Low retention.

A mobile CMUsed app to simplify the process.

From listing to messaging to the deal, on the phone. In progress.

From browsing to a deal.

The site as I found it, built by the original team. It follows the moments a buyer goes through, and each screen carries only what that moment needs.

  1. 01Browse.

    Two large ways in, clothing and furniture, then the most recent listings.

  2. 02Search and filter.

    Results beside filters for price, quality and category, with sorting on the right.

  3. 03Decide.

    The listing expands the card: more photos, price and whether it is negotiable, condition, when it was posted, who is selling, and one clear Contact Seller button.

  4. 04Message.

    Contact Seller opens a chat with the listing already attached and a first message written, so reaching out takes one send.

The card at a glance

Most of the time on CMUsed is spent scrolling through listings, so the card only carries what it takes to decide whether to look closer: a big photo, the item's condition, its category, the name and price, and the two things you can do next.

From mobile web to an app

On phones, CMUsed began as a mobile web prototype: the same site in the phone's browser, with the pieces stacked into a single column. We are now turning it into a mobile app, so browsing, messaging and selling work the way people expect on a phone.

Listing an item.

Selling is its own short flow, one tap away from Sell Now at the top of every page.
