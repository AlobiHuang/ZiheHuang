Kumi

One glance tells you what’s next.

A small window for the deadlines, events and messages spread across your apps.

Interactive prototype. The screens use sample data; real account connections are next.

At a glance

One board for the things you would otherwise check app by app.

  • One list across apps.

    Deadlines, events and replies together, ordered by when they are due.

  • Colour with a job.

    The hue tells you the kind. The amount of colour tells you the urgency.

  • Small enough to stay open.

    A window beside your work, with more detail as you make it larger.

01 Problem

A day spread across a dozen apps.

A student’s schedule, deadlines and messages live in different places. Knowing what is next means opening each app, and moving what is due into a calendar or to-do list by hand.

Kumi starts with that round of checking: the information already exists, but it is not together.

  1. Too many places to check.

    The calendar, inbox and course tools each hold a different part of the day.

  2. Switching just to get a picture.

    Understanding what is next means opening those apps one after another.

  3. Organizing the same information again.

    Deadlines get copied into another calendar or list.

02 Solution

One small window. Six ways to make the day clearer.

01

Needs you now, in colours that mean something.

Deadlines, events, replies and reminders sit in one list, soonest first. Kind is shown by a colour, icon and word; urgency changes how much colour an item carries. Done, Snooze and Dismiss come with Undo.

  • ClassCanvas, Todoist, deadlines
  • TimeCalendar, Zoom, gym bookings
  • PeopleGmail, iMessage, Slack
  • BodyHealth, gym, Grubhub
  • YoursYour notes and trackers
  • ToolsClaude, GitHub, Notion
  • NowSolid, outlined, a hard shadow
  • SoonA soft tint and a coloured edge
  • LaterPlain paper and a dot
02

A calendar that fills itself.

What is due sits under each day, in its colour. Events stay quieter below it. The design brings the same items from the board into a week view, without entering them a second time.

03

One small window, any size.

Three sizes, each in landscape or portrait. Widgets move and resize on a grid, showing more detail as they grow. At their smallest, they keep only the app, one number and a few words. Every drag also has a one-click or keyboard alternative.

The same inbox, from a glance to the whole list.

  • 1 × 1 · a glance
  • 2 × 1 · a tile
  • 2 × 2 · who's first
  • 3 × 3 · the whole list
04

Widgets from the apps you use.

Nineteen widgets in seven groups, each with a preview. The Sources page explains what each app would contribute and how it would connect. Connections are designed to be read-only; the prototype uses sample data.

05

Your own notes, when they matter.

A reminder can stay out of the way until the date you choose. Then it joins Needs you now alongside your other deadlines. Marking it done removes it from the list and calendar, with Undo available.

  1. 01Write it

    Dentist, Saturday 4:30 pm. Bring the insurance card.

  2. 02Hidden until it matters

    Show it 3 days before, so the board stays about now.

  3. 03Joins Needs you now

    In the week it’s due, sorted with every app’s deadlines.

  4. 04Done, with Undo

    Tick it off and it leaves the list and the calendar.

06

Wherever you look.

The same board as a read-only wallpaper, a dark window, or one column on a phone. The layouts change; the order and colour system stay familiar.

Designed with access in mind.

Kumi targets WCAG 2.2 AA, with keyboard operation, measured contrast in both themes, tables alongside charts, and Undo messages that do not time out. The project reports 132 automated tests; a screen-reader pass and testing with people are still next steps.

03 Process

Shaped by what didn’t work.

Three explorations behind the current design.

  • 01

    From a full screen to a window.

    The full-screen dashboard became a compact window beside your work.

  • 02

    One colour system, three styles.

    Gummy kept the warmth while giving urgent items the strongest emphasis.

  • 03

    Recognizable app icons.

    Kumi’s K takes each app’s shape, replacing the earlier abstract textures.

04 Reflection

What I’m taking forward.

A glance starts with what is left out.

The move from a full screen to a small window made the hierarchy more important. Each widget needs a useful smallest version, not a miniature of everything.

Colour needs a consistent meaning.

Kind and urgency do different jobs. Keeping that rule across the board, calendar and smaller widgets gives the colour a purpose.

Next steps

  1. 01Connect real accounts.

    Every app runs on realistic sample data for now. Next is sign-in and the real connections; iMessage, Grubhub and LinkedIn have no public API, so the Sources page says how each one would connect.

  2. 02Test with students.

    A week of real use with a few students, to see whether one glance really replaces the round of apps.

  3. 03A screen reader pass.

    A full pass with NVDA, then testing with people who use assistive technology every day.
